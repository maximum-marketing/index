Lesson 1 of 6

The Value Mindset

Why getting more from existing transactions is a service, not a sales tactic.

Reframing Upselling

Most business owners are uncomfortable with upselling because they associate it with pushy selling. But genuine upselling is the opposite -- ensuring clients know everything available to them that could solve their problems. A client who leaves without knowing you could have solved their second problem has been underserved, not protected.

The Gap Between What Clients Buy and What They Need

In most businesses there is a significant gap between what clients currently buy and what they genuinely need. They may be unaware of your full service range. They may not have connected their problems to your solutions. They may be buying individual services from multiple providers when you could serve all their needs better. Closing this gap is better client service -- not upselling.

💡 The Full Needs Audit

The most natural upsell conversation

The most effective way to increase average sale value is to understand the full scope of a client's needs at the start of the relationship. A 45-minute onboarding conversation that maps all their challenges and goals will naturally surface 2-3 additional service opportunities. This is thorough professional practice -- not selling.

What Your Numbers Tell You

If your average sale value has been flat for 12+ months you are leaving revenue on the table. If you are not actively upselling, bundling or upgrading clients, your average sale value is declining in real terms as inflation erodes your prices. Average sale value requires active management -- it does not grow by default.

Lesson 2 of 6

Pricing Strategies That Drive Higher Sale Values

How to structure your pricing to naturally drive higher transaction values.

  • 1

    Three-tier pricing -- Good, Better, Best

    When clients see three options most choose the middle tier -- which you price as your preferred option. The premium tier makes the middle look reasonable. The entry tier reassures clients they have a choice. Businesses switching from single-price to three-tier typically see average sale value increase 15-25%.

  • 2

    Price for outcomes not inputs

    Hourly pricing caps your revenue at your time. Outcome-based pricing -- a set fee for a defined result -- means your efficiency becomes profit. "We will get you to page 1 of Google for these 10 keywords for $2,200/month" is more compelling and more profitable than charging by the hour.

  • 3

    Bundle to increase perceived value and actual price

    A bundle of three services priced at $2,800 feels like better value than three services individually totalling $2,400. The client pays more but perceives more value. Combine complementary services, name the bundle compellingly, and price it above the sum of individual services.

  • 4

    Introduce annual prepayment with a discount

    Offer 10% off for 12 months upfront. You get better cash flow and guaranteed revenue. The client gets a saving. Clients who prepay stay -- switching costs are higher when they have already paid.

  • 5

    Charge for value-adds you currently give away

    Quick consultations, minor amendments, reporting, ad hoc advice -- formalise these. Either include them in a premium tier or charge for them. Time you give away is revenue you never see.

  • 6

    Review and increase prices annually

    A business that increases prices 5% annually has prices 28% higher after 5 years. A business that never increases has prices that have declined significantly in real terms. Build your annual price review into your business calendar as non-negotiable.

📍 Three-Tier Pricing in Practice

Marketing Agency Example

Entry $1,400/month: SEO only, monthly report, email support.
Standard $2,200/month: SEO + Google Ads, weekly report, monthly call, phone support.
Premium $3,400/month: Everything plus social media strategy, competitor monitoring, quarterly business review.

Before introducing this: avg client value $1,800/month. After 6 months: avg client value $2,350/month. 30% increase with the same clients.

Lesson 3 of 6

Upselling and Cross-Selling Systems

Building the conversations, scripts and incentives that make selling more natural and consistent.

Systems Beat Willpower

The most common reason businesses fail to upsell is not lack of desire -- it is lack of system. When upselling depends on someone remembering to mention additional services, it happens inconsistently. When it is built into every client interaction as a standard process, it happens every time.

  • 1

    Map every natural upsell in your client journey

    For each point in your client relationship -- onboarding, monthly delivery, quarterly review, annual renewal -- what upsell is naturally relevant at that moment? Document these. Build them into your standard process.

  • 2

    Create upsell scripts for your team

    Scripts ensure the right conversation happens every time. "Since you are getting great results from SEO, many clients find Google Ads dramatically accelerates growth -- would you like me to put a proposal together?" Practice this. Make it natural. It converts.

  • 3

    Align incentives with average sale value

    If staff are paid on revenue they sell whatever is easiest, not what is most valuable. Consider bonuses tied to average sale value, lifetime value or add-ons sold.

  • 4

    Use quarterly reviews to surface new needs

    A structured quarterly review agenda should include results delivered, goals for next quarter, and any new challenges or opportunities. The last item naturally surfaces service opportunities. Most clients appreciate being asked.

  • 5

    Track upsell rate as a KPI

    What percentage of your clients buy more than one service? Track this monthly. A client who has been with you 12 months and only buys one service is a missed opportunity.

  • 6

    Make it easy to say yes

    A one-page add-on proposal with clear outcomes, clear price and simple sign-off converts dramatically better than a full formal proposal process.

💡 The Add-On Menu

Give clients a simple way to buy more

Create a one-page add-on services document listing everything available beyond the core service. Price each item clearly. Include it in every quarterly review. Businesses that implement an add-on menu typically see 20-40% of clients add at least one service within the first quarter.

Lesson 4 of 6

Staff Training for Higher Sale Values

Building a team that naturally sells more without it feeling like selling.

Your Team Is Your Sales Force

Every person who interacts with clients is either increasing or decreasing your average sale value. A staff member who never mentions additional services is costing you revenue just as surely as one who actively discourages upselling. The difference between a team that naturally increases average sale value and one that does not is training and incentives -- not personality.

  • 1

    Train upselling as a customer service skill

    Reframe upselling to your team as "making sure clients know everything we can do for them." This mindset shift is significant. Staff who see upselling as customer service perform it more consistently than those who see it as selling.

  • 2

    Practice scripts in team meetings

    15 minutes of script practice in a monthly team meeting -- role-play the upsell conversation -- dramatically improves consistency and confidence. The scripts become natural through repetition.

  • 3

    Create a daily upsell target and track it

    Even a simple target -- every client interaction should include one mention of an additional service -- creates accountability. Track it. Review it. Celebrate wins.

  • 4

    Incentivise add-ons and upgrades

    A $50-100 bonus for each client upgrade costs very little relative to the ongoing revenue generated. A staff member who upgrades a client from $2,200 to $3,400/month has generated $14,400 in additional annual revenue. A $100 bonus is an excellent return.

  • 5

    Share average sale value data with the team monthly

    Teams that see the numbers perform better than teams that do not. Share average sale value, add-ons sold, upgrade conversions at every monthly meeting.

  • 6

    Hire and review for selling capability

    When hiring, assess whether candidates can naturally have conversations about additional services. Include average sale value contribution as a performance metric alongside delivery quality.

💡 The Weekly Upsell Meeting

15 minutes that pays

Every Monday, 15 minutes: (1) Review client interactions -- any upsell opportunities missed? (2) Practice one script for the most common upsell. (3) Set one upsell target per team member for the week. This meeting costs nothing, takes 15 minutes, and directly drives average sale value improvement.
