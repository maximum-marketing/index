What is the Marketing Mix?

The four Ps framework — a practical tool for building and reviewing your marketing strategy.

The Four Ps Framework

The Marketing Mix — also known as the 4 Ps — is one of the most widely used frameworks in marketing strategy. First introduced by E. Jerome McCarthy in the 1960s, it remains relevant because it forces you to think about your business from four different but interconnected angles: Product, Price, Promotion and Place.

What you sell

🛠️ Product

Your product or service — its features, quality, design, branding, packaging and what makes it different.

What you charge

💰 Price

Your pricing strategy — how much you charge, payment terms, discounting, bundling and perceived value.

How you reach customers

📢 Promotion

How you communicate with the market — advertising, SEO, social media, referrals, events, content.

Where you sell

📍 Place

Where and how customers access your product — location, online, distribution channels, convenience.

💡 The Integration Principle

The 4 Ps only work when they are consistent with each other

A premium product should have premium pricing and premium promotion in premium channels. If you have an excellent product but budget pricing and low-quality promotion, the signals confuse the market. Every P should tell the same story about your business.

Lesson 2 of 6

Product and Price — Building Your Offer

How to define what you're selling and price it for maximum value and margin.

The Product P — Beyond the Basics

Your product or service is more than what it does. It is the entire experience a client has when they buy from you — from the first quote to the final delivery. For service businesses, your "product" includes: how you communicate, how you report, how you onboard, what guarantees you offer and what the client walks away feeling. All of these are part of your product.

  • 1

    Define your core offer precisely

    Can you describe your service in one specific sentence? "Monthly SEO management that gets Brisbane trade businesses to page 1 of Google within 6 months, with weekly reporting and a dedicated account manager."

  • 2

    Identify your product differentiators

    What three things about your service are genuinely different from competitors? These are your product features that justify your price and win in sales conversations.

  • 3

    Consider add-ons and bundles

    What could you add to your core service to increase value and average revenue? A monthly strategy call. A competitor report. A quarterly website audit. These often cost you little but significantly increase perceived value.

  • 4

    Review and improve regularly

    Survey your best clients every 6 months. What do they love? What would make the service better? Your product should evolve based on real client feedback, not assumptions.

The Price P — Value, Not Cost

The most common pricing mistake is setting prices based on your costs rather than on the value you deliver. If your SEO generates $50,000 in additional revenue for a client, charging $2,000 a month is excellent value — not a significant expense. Price based on outcomes delivered, not hours worked.

💡 Four Pricing Strategies to Know

Premium, Competitive, Penetration and Value-Based

Premium pricing: charge more than competitors, justify with superior product/service. Competitive: match market rates, win on relationship and service. Penetration: start below market to gain clients, increase later. Value-based: price relative to the financial outcome you deliver — the most profitable strategy for service businesses when you can prove ROI.

Promotion — How to Get in Front of the Right People

The promotion channels that work for Australian small businesses, and how to choose the right ones.

Promotion is Not Just Advertising

Promotion covers every way you communicate with your market: digital advertising, SEO, social media, direct sales, referral programs, events, PR, email marketing, content and partnerships. Most small businesses underuse at least 4 of these channels and overuse 1 or 2.

  • 1

    Define your promotion goal first

    Are you trying to build awareness, generate leads, nurture existing leads, or retain clients? Different goals need different channels. Mixing them without clarity leads to unfocused spend.

  • 2

    Identify where your ideal clients actually spend time

    B2B clients spend time on LinkedIn and in industry associations. Tradies' target homeowners spend time on Facebook and Google. Retail customers find businesses on Instagram and Google Maps. Go where your clients are — not where you are comfortable.

  • 3

    Build a referral program — the highest ROI channel

    Word of mouth is already your best source of business. A structured referral program makes it systematic. Offer a meaningful incentive, make it easy to refer, and ask for referrals at the right moment — when a client has just had a great result.

  • 4

    Choose 2–3 channels and do them well

    The biggest promotion mistake is doing 6 channels poorly. Pick 2–3 channels that reach your ideal client where they actually are. Be consistent. Be patient. Most channels take 3–6 months to generate results.

  • 5

    Measure everything

    Cost per lead, conversion rate, revenue per channel. Know which promotion investment is generating return and which is not. Review monthly and reallocate budget accordingly.

📍 The Brisbane Small Business Promotion Stack

What works for most service businesses in SEQ

Google Ads (immediate leads), Google Business Profile (local search), SEO (medium-term organic), LinkedIn (B2B), Facebook (B2C), Email marketing (retention and referral). For most service businesses, Google + referral program + monthly email newsletter is the highest ROI combination to start with.

Lesson 4 of 6

Place — Where and How Clients Access You

The distribution and delivery decisions that affect how easy you are to buy from.

Place is About Accessibility and Convenience

In the 4 Ps framework, "Place" originally referred to physical distribution. For service businesses, it means: how do clients access and experience your service? Online or in-person? Self-serve or full service? Local or national? The right "place" strategy makes it as easy as possible for the right clients to buy from you.

For Service Businesses, Place Means...

Where do you deliver your service (onsite, remote, hybrid)? How do clients engage with you (phone, email, booking system, walk-in)? What is your geographic reach? How do they pay and what terms? All of these affect how easy you are to buy from.

  • 1

    Map your current client journey

    From first contact to signed contract — how many steps are there? Where do prospects drop off? Every unnecessary step in your buying process loses clients.

  • 2

    Make it easier to buy from you

    Can a prospect book a call directly from your website? Can they sign a proposal digitally? Can they pay online? Friction in the purchase process costs you clients who would otherwise have signed.

  • 3

    Consider your geographic strategy

    Are you local only, or could you serve interstate clients remotely? Many service businesses (marketing, accounting, legal, coaching) have expanded significantly by removing the geographic constraint.

  • 4

    Review your hours and availability

    If you are only reachable during business hours and your competitors have online booking or 24-hour quote requests, you are losing business to availability alone.

💡 The Friction Audit

Every step in your buying process that requires client effort loses a percentage of prospects

A client who has to email for information, wait for a reply, call to discuss, wait for a quote and then print and sign a PDF has been given 5 opportunities to choose a competitor who makes it easier. Book a meeting with yourself: go through your own buying process as if you were a new prospect and count every point of friction.
