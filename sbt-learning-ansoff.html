What is the Ansoff Matrix?

A simple but powerful framework that maps your growth options against risk.

The Growth Strategy Framework

The Ansoff Matrix was developed by Igor Ansoff and first published in 1957. It gives you four clear growth strategies based on two questions: Are you selling existing or new products? Are you selling to existing or new markets? Every possible growth strategy your business could pursue fits into one of these four boxes.

Lowest Risk

Market Penetration

Sell more of your existing products to your existing customers. The safest growth strategy.

Medium Risk

Product Development

Develop new products or services for your existing customers who already know and trust you.

Medium Risk

Market Development

Take your existing products into new markets — new suburbs, new industries, new demographics.

Highest Risk

Diversification

Launch new products into new markets. The riskiest strategy — but can deliver the highest rewards.

💡 Why This Matters

Risk increases as you move away from what you know

Market Penetration is safest because you know the product and the customer. Diversification is riskiest because you know neither. Most small businesses should focus 70% of their growth energy on Market Penetration and Product Development before attempting the riskier quadrants.

Lesson 2 of 6

Market Penetration — Grow with What You Have

The lowest risk growth strategy. Sell more of what you already sell to people who already buy from you.

What is Market Penetration?

Market penetration means increasing your sales of existing products to your existing customers and market. You are not changing your product and you are not finding completely new markets. You are simply getting more from what you already have. This is where most small businesses should focus first.

  • 1

    Increase purchase frequency

    How do you get existing customers to buy more often? Loyalty programs, regular check-ins, service reminders, subscription models. A lawn mowing client who comes every 4 weeks could become a fortnightly client.

  • 2

    Increase average transaction value

    Upsell, cross-sell, bundle. If a client buys SEO, do they need Google Ads? Content writing? If a client buys cleaning, do they need carpet cleaning? Bundled pricing increases value without finding new customers.

  • 3

    Reactivate lost customers

    Your old clients already know and trust you. A simple phone call or email to lapsed clients is often the fastest way to generate revenue — and they convert at far higher rates than cold prospects.

  • 4

    Win competitor clients

    If you are in the same market as competitors, their unhappy clients are your warmest prospects. Google reviews, referral programs and direct outreach to known competitor clients can drive significant growth.

  • 5

    Increase market share through price or positioning

    Become more competitive on price for certain segments, or reposition to command a premium. Both strategies increase your share of the existing market.

💡 Brisbane Example

A commercial cleaning company penetrating their existing market

Instead of looking for new suburbs to service, they called every lapsed client from the past 2 years, offered a free spring clean, and converted 30% into regular contracts. Revenue up 22% without a single new marketing dollar spent.

Lesson 3 of 6

Product Development & Market Development

Moving into adjacent growth — still manageable risk if done right.

Product Development — New Products, Same Customers

You develop new products or services for people who already trust and buy from you. Because the customer relationship is established, the risk is lower than finding new markets. The key is listening to your existing customers — what do they keep asking for that you don't currently offer?

Market Development — Same Products, New Markets

You take your existing products into new markets — new geographic areas, new industries, new age groups or demographics. The risk is that you know the product but not the new customer. Research is critical before committing resources.

📍 Brisbane Case Studies

Product Development: Marketing agency adds LinkedIn ads

An SEO agency noticed its B2B clients kept asking about LinkedIn advertising. Instead of referring them elsewhere, they trained up, added LinkedIn Ads as a service, and within 6 months it was 30% of revenue — all from existing clients.

Market Development: Plumber expands to Gold Coast

A Brisbane plumber had maxed out their market. Rather than change their service, they hired a Gold Coast-based subcontractor, ran targeted Google Ads in GC postcodes, and within a year had 40% of revenue from a market they didn't exist in 18 months earlier.

💡 The Key Question for Both

Before entering either quadrant, ask one question

Product Development: Do my existing customers actually want this — or do I just think they do? Market Development: Do I understand the new customer well enough to serve them profitably? Both strategies punish assumptions. Research first, invest second.

Lesson 4 of 6

Diversification — The High Risk, High Reward Play

When to diversify, when not to, and how to reduce the risk if you do.

What is Diversification?

Diversification means launching entirely new products into entirely new markets. It is the highest risk quadrant because you are operating without the safety net of either an existing product or an existing customer base. Both are unknown. But done right, it is also where the biggest opportunities live — particularly if your current market is shrinking or commoditising.

  • 1

    Related Diversification — lower risk

    Add something connected to what you already do. A lawn mowing company that adds landscaping design. An accountant who adds financial planning. The new product uses existing skills or the new market has similar characteristics to your existing one.

  • 2

    Unrelated Diversification — high risk

    Completely new product, completely new market. This is usually driven by opportunity (an investment, a partnership, an acquisition) rather than organic growth. Success requires significant capital and management bandwidth.

  • 3

    Test before you commit

    Never fully commit to diversification without a minimum viable test. Sell the new product to 3 customers. Enter the new market in one suburb. Run a trial for 90 days. If it works, scale. If it doesn't, you've lost 90 days not 2 years.

  • 4

    When diversification is the right call

    Declining market, technology disrupting your current product, seasonal revenue that needs stabilising, a clear and researched opportunity that your existing skills give you an edge in. Diversification out of boredom or fear is almost always a mistake.

⚠️ The Most Common Mistake

Diversifying before mastering what you have

Most small businesses diversify too early — before they've fully penetrated their existing market, before their product development pipeline is full, before their systems are strong enough to handle more complexity. Build a strong foundation first. Diversification is a growth accelerator, not a business rescue strategy.
