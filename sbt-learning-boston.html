What is the Boston Matrix?

A strategic tool that sorts your products and services by growth and market share — so you know where to invest and what to cut.

The Product Portfolio Framework

The Boston Matrix — developed by the Boston Consulting Group — is a tool for analysing your portfolio of products or services. It plots each offering against two axes: market growth rate and your relative market share. The result is four categories, each with a different strategic prescription.

High Growth · High Share

⭐ STAR

High-growth service with strong market share. Invest and protect. These are your future Cash Cows.

High Growth · Low Share

❓ QUESTION MARK

Fast-growing market but you have low share. Big potential — but needs investment to decide if it becomes a Star.

Low Growth · High Share

🐄 CASH COW

Your reliable revenue engine. Market is mature but you dominate. Generate cash here to fund your Stars.

Low Growth · Low Share

🐕 DOG

Low growth, low share. Draining resources without reward. Consider eliminating or repositioning these.

💡 Why This Matters for Small Business

You have limited time, money and energy — put them in the right place

Most small businesses have at least one Dog they keep out of habit or emotion, one Cash Cow they take for granted, and one Question Mark they haven't decided about. This matrix forces you to make those decisions explicitly rather than by default.

Lesson 2 of 6

How to Plot Your Products

Step by step — mapping each product or service onto the matrix.

  • 1

    List every product and service you offer

    Write them all down. Include things you do occasionally or reluctantly. Include that service you keep meaning to drop. Everything goes on the list first.

  • 2

    Assess market growth rate for each

    Is the demand for this type of service growing, flat or declining? You don't need exact numbers — think high growth (expanding market), medium (stable) or low growth (contracting or saturated).

  • 3

    Assess your relative market share

    Are you the dominant player, a strong player, or a small player in this category? Relative to your direct local competitors — not the whole country.

  • 4

    Plot on the matrix

    Place each service in the most accurate quadrant. Be honest. A service that feels like a Star might actually be a Question Mark if your market share is genuinely low.

  • 5

    Identify the strategic implications

    Stars: protect and invest. Cash Cows: maintain efficiently. Question Marks: decide — invest to grow or divest. Dogs: cut or reposition. Document what action you will take for each.

  • 6

    Review every 6 months

    Markets change. Your Star of today can become a Cash Cow as growth slows. Your Question Mark can become a Star with the right investment. This is not a one-time exercise.

📍 Brisbane Example — Digital Marketing Agency

How a small agency applied the Boston Matrix

SEO Retainer (Cash Cow): stable market, agency dominates locally. Strategy: maintain, don't over-invest.

Google Ads (Star): fast-growing demand, agency has strong share from early mover advantage. Strategy: invest in capability and promotion.

Social Media Management (Question Mark): market growing fast but agency is one of dozens. Strategy: decide within 90 days — niche down or exit.

One-off Website Builds (Dog): saturated market, low margin, no recurring revenue. Strategy: stop taking new projects, migrate existing clients to retainer model.

Lesson 3 of 6

Stars and Question Marks — Where to Invest

How to nurture your growth services and make clear decisions on potential.

Managing Your Stars ⭐

Stars are high-growth, high-share services. They are exciting and often your best client acquisition tool — but they are also resource-hungry. The mistake most businesses make with Stars is under-investing because the Cash Cow is generating comfortable revenue. Stars need investment in capability, marketing and capacity or they will lose market share as the market grows.

Managing Your Question Marks ❓

Question Marks require a binary decision. You invest to make them Stars, or you exit. The worst thing you can do is leave them in limbo — consuming resources without a clear path to strong market share. The decision framework: Do you have the resources and capability to genuinely win market share? If yes, commit. If no, redirect those resources to your Stars.

📍 Case Study — Question Mark Decision

Should the agency invest in social media management?

The agency had been offering social media management for 2 years but was one of 50+ agencies in Brisbane offering the same service with similar pricing. They ran the Question Mark framework:

Can we win meaningful market share? Not without significant investment in a niche.
What would it take? Specialise in one industry (e.g. trades) and become the known agency for trades social media.
Decision: Niche down. Within 12 months they were the leading social media agency for Brisbane trade businesses — Question Mark became a Star.

💡 The Question Mark Decision Framework

Three questions that force a clear decision

1. Can we realistically achieve strong market share in 12–18 months?
2. Do we have — or can we build — a genuine competitive advantage?
3. Are we willing to commit the resources it actually takes?

If the answer to any of these is no, exit the quadrant. The resources are better deployed elsewhere.

Lesson 4 of 6

Cash Cows and Dogs — Protect and Prune

How to milk your reliable revenue streams and cut what's draining you.

Managing Your Cash Cows 🐄

Cash Cows are your most important asset — but they are also the most taken for granted. They generate reliable revenue with relatively low investment. The strategic goal is to maintain market share efficiently while extracting maximum cash to fund Stars and Question Mark decisions. Don't kill your Cash Cow by under-servicing it or by diverting too many resources away.

The Dog Trap 🐕

Dogs are the hardest to deal with emotionally. They are often services you started with, that you enjoy doing, or that represent long-standing client relationships. But they drain resources that should be going to Stars. The discipline to cut Dogs — or at minimum stop actively investing in them — is one of the most important strategic decisions a small business owner can make.

  • 1

    Cash Cow: systematise and protect

    Build processes that deliver your Cash Cow service efficiently. Protect client relationships. Don't discount — mature markets often allow premium pricing if you have strong market share.

  • 2

    Cash Cow: resist the temptation to neglect it

    As Stars and Question Marks grab your attention, your Cash Cow can suffer. Set minimum service standards. Review Cash Cow clients quarterly. Their loyalty is not guaranteed.

  • 3

    Dog: stop actively marketing it

    Don't spend money acquiring new Dog clients. Serve existing ones well but don't grow the category.

  • 4

    Dog: migrate clients to better services

    Can your Dog clients transition to a Cash Cow or Star service? A one-off website build client could become an SEO retainer client.

  • 5

    Dog: set a deadline

    Give yourself 6 months. Either the Dog turns around or it goes. No indefinite limbo.

💡 The Hardest Question

Which Dog are you keeping out of habit, not strategy?

Be honest. There is almost always one. It might be a service type, a client segment or even a geographic market. The resources tied up in your Dog are the resources your Star is starving for.
