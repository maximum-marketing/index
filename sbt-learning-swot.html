Lesson 1 of 6

What is a SWOT Analysis?

The tool that forces you to look at your business honestly — from the inside and the outside.

The Big Picture

SWOT stands for Strengths, Weaknesses, Opportunities and Threats. It's a structured way of looking at where your business stands right now and what's happening around it — so you can make smarter decisions about where to go next.

S

Strengths

Internal advantages your business has over competitors.

W

Weaknesses

Internal areas where your business is vulnerable.

O

Opportunities

External changes in the market you could take advantage of.

T

Threats

External forces that could damage your business.

💡 The Crucial Distinction

Internal vs External

Strengths and Weaknesses are internal — within your control. Opportunities and Threats are external — in the market around you. A new competitor is a Threat, not a Weakness. Your slow quoting process is a Weakness, not a Threat.

Why Most SWOTs Fail

Most business owners fill in a SWOT once, put it in a drawer and forget it. A SWOT only works if it leads to a decision. By the end of this module you'll know exactly how to turn your SWOT into 3 clear actions.

Lesson 2 of 6

How to Build Your SWOT

Step by step — from blank page to a completed analysis you can actually use.

  • 1

    Set the context first

    Be clear on what you're analysing before you start — your whole business, a specific service, or a new market. Without scope, your SWOT becomes too vague to generate useful strategy.

  • 2

    Strengths — be specific

    Don't write "good customer service." Write "97% of Google reviews mention our response time." Vague strengths lead to vague strategies.

  • 3

    Be brutal about Weaknesses

    Your weaknesses are what your best competitors do better than you. Ask customers. Ask staff who've recently left. A weakness you ignore is a threat you can't see coming.

  • 4

    Research Opportunities actively

    Look at: new technology, changing customer habits, gaps left by competitors who've closed, new industrial areas, government grants. Opportunities hide in plain sight.

  • 5

    Face Threats honestly

    New competitors, price pressure, regulations, economic slowdowns, technology disrupting your industry. The threats you don't acknowledge are the ones that catch you off guard.

  • 6

    Turn it into strategy

    Ask: How do I use strengths to capture opportunities? Fix weaknesses before threats exploit them? Use strengths to neutralise threats? Fix weaknesses to open up opportunities?

💡 Don't Do This Alone

Get 2–3 perspectives

A SWOT done by only the business owner has blind spots. Get your best staff member, your accountant or a trusted customer to contribute. You'll be surprised at what they see that you don't.

Lesson 3 of 6

A Real Business Example

How a Brisbane trades business completed their SWOT — and the 3 decisions that came from it.

📍 Case Study — Brisbane Electrical, 8 years, 3 staff

Feeling pressure from a national franchise moving in. Didn't know how to respond.

✓ Strengths
  • 62 Google reviews 4.9★
  • Owner attends every job
  • Same-week availability
  • Strong builder relationships
✗ Weaknesses
  • No website at all
  • Can't take large jobs
  • Owner does all admin
  • No social media
→ Opportunities
  • New industrial estate opening
  • Two competitors closed
  • EV charger installs booming
  • Government solar rebates
⚠ Threats
  • National franchise moving in
  • Apprentice shortage
  • Rising material costs
  • Builder contacts may retire
📈 The 3 Decisions From This SWOT

Cross-category combinations

1. Strength + Opportunity: Use 62 reviews to build a website and capture the market left by two closed competitors.

2. Opportunity + Weakness: Register as EV charger installer before the franchise gets established in that niche.

3. Weakness + Threat: Hire apprentice now — fixes capacity weakness and reduces vulnerability to franchise.

The pattern that creates strategy

Every decision came from combining a SWOT factor across categories. This cross-combination is what turns a SWOT from a document into a strategy.

Lesson 4 of 6

5 Mistakes That Kill a SWOT

Avoid these and your SWOT will outperform most of what gets done in businesses twice your size.

❌ Mistake 1 — Being too vague

"Good service" is not a strength. "We respond to quotes within 2 hours — competitors take 2 days" is a strength. Force yourself to be specific enough that someone outside your business could verify it.

❌ Mistake 2 — Doing it alone

A SWOT done solo has blind spots. Your staff member sees things you don't. Your accountant sees numbers differently. A customer sees you through a buyer's eyes. Get 2–3 perspectives.

❌ Mistake 3 — Confusing internal and external

"A new competitor opened nearby" is a Threat — not a Weakness. Mixing them up leads to completely wrong strategies.

❌ Mistake 4 — Not prioritising

A SWOT with 20 items in each box is useless. Force yourself to your top 3–5 in each category. Priority drives focus — and focus is what small businesses need most.

❌ Mistake 5 — Never connecting it to a decision

Every SWOT session must end with at least 3 specific decisions. Not "we should look at marketing" but "by end of month we will launch Google Ads targeting [keyword] in [suburb]."

💡 Make It Quarterly

60 minutes, 4 times a year

Market conditions change, competitors move, your business evolves. The businesses that review their SWOT every quarter consistently outperform those that do it once and forget it.
