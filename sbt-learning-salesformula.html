The Three Sales Levers

Why working on all three drivers simultaneously produces results no single strategy can match.

The Formula

Every business generates revenue through exactly three variables: how much each customer spends (average sale value), how often they buy (purchase frequency), and how many customers you have. Small improvements to each variable compound multiplicatively — a 10% improvement on each does not produce 10% growth. It produces 33%.

The Sales Growth Formula
Average Sale Value × Purchase Frequency × Number of Customers = Revenue
Improve each lever by 10% and total revenue grows by 33.1%

💡 The Most Overlooked Lever

Acquisition is the most expensive growth strategy

Most businesses spend 80% of sales effort on finding new customers and almost nothing on increasing what existing customers spend or how often they buy. Acquiring a new customer costs 5-7x more than selling to an existing one. The formula shows exactly why this leaves enormous revenue on the table.

Lesson 2 of 6

Increasing Average Sale Value

How to get more revenue from every transaction without finding new customers.

Three Core Strategies

There are three proven ways to increase average sale value: raise your prices, create premium tiers that give clients a reason to spend more, and bundle complementary services so clients pay more for greater convenience and value. Each strategy works on its own — together they are transformational.

  • 1

    Raise your prices — the fastest lever

    If you have not raised prices in 12 months you have absorbed cost increases and compressed your margin. A 5-10% price increase communicated professionally is accepted by 85-90% of clients. The revenue lost from the 10-15% who leave rarely justifies keeping prices low for everyone.

  • 2

    Create a premium tier

    Bundle your best services at a higher price point. Clients who want the best will pay for it. Those who want basics stay at standard pricing. Premium tiers typically generate 30-50% higher revenue per client with minimal additional cost.

  • 3

    Bundle complementary services

    Package services that naturally complement each other at a price that feels like value but is higher than the sum of parts. An SEO agency bundles SEO + Google Ads + reporting into one package. The client pays more and perceives more value.

  • 4

    Add premium add-ons to your core service

    A monthly strategy call. A competitor report. Priority response. Express delivery. These cost you relatively little but meaningfully increase average revenue per client.

  • 5

    Stop giving away value for free

    Many businesses provide informal extras they never bill for — quick calls, minor amendments, ad hoc advice. Formalise these into your pricing or a premium tier. Time you give away is revenue you never capture.

  • 6

    Review your pricing model

    Are you charging by the hour when you should charge by outcome? Outcome-based pricing allows your efficiency to become profit rather than a discount for the client.

📍 Brisbane Example

How a premium tier added $78k in 12 months

14 clients on $1,800/month SEO. Introduced: (1) 6% price increase — 13 of 14 accepted: +$11,232/year. (2) Premium tier at $2,800 including Google Ads — 4 clients upgraded: +$48,000/year. (3) Competitor report add-on at $300/month — 6 clients: +$21,600/year. Total: $80,832 additional from existing clients. Zero new acquisition required.

Lesson 3 of 6

Increasing Transaction Frequency

Getting existing customers to buy more often — the most underused sales strategy.

Your Existing Customers Are Your Best Asset

An existing customer already knows you, trusts you and has demonstrated willingness to pay. Marketing to them costs a fraction of new customer acquisition. Yet most businesses invest almost nothing in systematically encouraging existing customers to buy again. Transaction frequency is typically the highest-return lever — and the least invested in.

  • 1

    Build a regular communication system

    Customers who hear from you regularly buy more often. A monthly email newsletter, a quarterly check-in call, a regular update email — any consistent touchpoint that reminds your customer you exist. Consistent is the key word. One email a year does nothing. Monthly contact changes behaviour significantly.

  • 2

    Create seasonal reasons to come back

    Promotions, new service launches, seasonal specials and exclusive client offers create specific moments that trigger a purchase decision. Plan 4-6 "reasons to come back" events per year and communicate them to your existing client base proactively.

  • 3

    Map adjacent services your clients naturally need

    What do your clients need alongside what they already buy from you? If you provide SEO, they need Google Ads, content, social. If you do accounting, they need financial planning. If you do cleaning, they need carpet and window cleaning. Map adjacent needs and make sure clients know you solve them.

  • 4

    Introduce accessory and complementary products

    Sell the accessories alongside the main product. Train staff to mention complementary services at every transaction. Build these into your quoting and proposal process as standard items.

  • 5

    Build a quarterly client review into your service

    A structured quarterly review surfaces new needs, demonstrates your value, and protects retention. A client who feels genuinely looked after and understands your results will buy more and refer more.

  • 6

    Sequence your service offering

    Design your range so each purchase naturally leads to the next. Every service should have a logical next step — and you should be proactively offering it at the right moment.

💡 The Monthly Newsletter

The highest-ROI marketing activity most businesses never do

A monthly email to existing clients takes 2 hours and costs nothing. It keeps you front of mind, positions you as an expert, surfaces clients ready to buy more, and generates referrals. Open rates from existing clients are 35-55% — far higher than cold marketing. Fewer than 20% of small businesses do this consistently. Start this week.

Lesson 4 of 6

Increasing Customer Count — Smarter Acquisition

How to grow your customer base efficiently without wasting marketing budget.

Acquisition Comes Third for a Reason

Before investing heavily in customer acquisition, optimise average sale value and transaction frequency. A business that converts well, retains clients and generates referrals needs far fewer new leads to hit growth targets than one relying purely on volume acquisition. Get the first two levers working, then scale the third.

  • 1

    Define your ideal customer precisely

    Most businesses waste acquisition budget targeting everyone. Define your ideal customer with specificity: industry, size, location, pain point, budget, decision maker. Every acquisition dollar works harder when aimed at the right person.

  • 2

    Build a referral program first

    A referred customer converts at 3-5x the rate of a cold lead, costs a fraction to acquire, and has higher lifetime value. A structured referral program with a meaningful incentive, a clear ask, and regular prompting is the highest-return acquisition investment most small businesses can make.

  • 3

    Improve conversion before increasing leads

    If you convert 25% of proposals you have two options: get 4x more leads, or improve conversion to 50% and achieve the same result. Conversion improvement is almost always faster and cheaper. Measure and improve your conversion rate before spending more on marketing.

  • 4

    Focus on 2-3 channels where your clients actually are

    Stop spreading thin across every channel. Your ideal clients are concentrated in 2-3 specific places. For most B2B service businesses: Google (SEO + Ads), LinkedIn, and industry associations. Be consistently present there.

  • 5

    Train staff on telephone and in-person scripts

    How enquiries are handled determines conversion. A structured phone script that qualifies, builds rapport and books an appointment converts at significantly higher rates than ad hoc responses. Practice scripts in team meetings monthly.

  • 6

    Measure cost per acquired client — not cost per lead

    A cheap lead that never converts is more expensive than a costly lead that always does. Track where each new client comes from and what it cost. Cost per acquired client — not cost per lead — is the metric that matters.

📍 The Referral Program That Doubled Acquisition

A Brisbane business coach's result

She emailed all 22 existing clients offering $300 account credit per referral who signed up. Result: 11 referrals in 6 months, 7 converted. Cost: $2,100. Revenue from 7 new clients at $2,000/month over 12 months: $168,000. Most cost-effective acquisition she had ever done.
