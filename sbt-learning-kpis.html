What is a Strategy Map?

How to visualise your business strategy so it drives decisions — not just decorates a wall.

Strategy Made Visual

A strategy map is a one-page visual that shows how your business activities connect to your financial goals. It was developed by Kaplan and Norton as part of the Balanced Scorecard framework and is now used by businesses of every size. The core idea: your financial results are the outcome of everything else you do — customer satisfaction, internal processes, and learning and growth.

Financial

Increase Revenue · Improve Profit Margins · Grow Cash Reserves

↑ driven by
Customer

Satisfaction · Retention · Referrals · New Client Acquisition

↑ driven by
Internal Processes

Service Delivery · Quoting Speed · Systems · Communication

↑ driven by
Learning and Growth

Training · New Services · Systems Investment · Culture

💡 The Cause and Effect Chain

Every financial result has a chain of causes beneath it

Want more revenue? You need more clients or higher value clients. To get more clients, you need better referrals or better marketing. To get better referrals, you need higher client satisfaction. To get higher satisfaction, you need better internal processes. To improve processes, you need training and systems. See the chain? That is a strategy map.

Lesson 2 of 6

Choosing the Right KPIs

Most businesses measure the wrong things. Here's how to choose the 8–12 numbers that actually tell you if you're on track.

Lead vs Lag Indicators

There are two types of KPIs. Lag indicators measure outcomes that have already happened — revenue, profit, number of clients. Lead indicators measure activities that predict future outcomes — number of proposals sent, conversion rate, client satisfaction score. Most businesses only measure lag indicators. But by the time a lag indicator is bad, it is already too late. Lead indicators give you early warning.

  • 1

    Financial KPIs — lag indicators

    Monthly revenue vs target, gross profit margin, net profit, monthly recurring revenue, cash in bank, debtor days. These tell you how you did.

  • 2

    Customer KPIs — mix of lead and lag

    Number of active clients, client retention rate, referrals received per month, new enquiries, average client value, NPS (Net Promoter Score). These tell you how your clients feel.

  • 3

    Internal Process KPIs — lead indicators

    Quote turnaround time, proposal conversion rate, project delivery on time, number of client complaints, average response time to enquiries. These predict your future customer metrics.

  • 4

    Learning and Growth KPIs — lead indicators

    Hours of training per month, number of new services developed, staff satisfaction, system improvements completed. These predict your future process metrics.

  • 5

    Choose 8–12 total — not more

    More than 12 KPIs and nothing gets the focus it needs. Be ruthless. Choose the metrics most directly connected to your strategy and your current biggest challenges.

📊 The MMS Recommended KPI Set for Service Businesses

A starting point you can customise

Financial (4): Monthly revenue, Gross margin %, Monthly recurring revenue, Cash balance
Customer (3): Active clients, Retention rate, Referrals received

Building Your Monthly Scorecard

A simple, consistent review process that keeps you on track every month.

What a Scorecard Looks Like

A monthly scorecard is a simple document — one page maximum — that shows each of your 12 KPIs with: the target, the actual result, the variance, and a RAG status (Red/Amber/Green). Green = on track. Amber = 5–10% off. Red = more than 10% off target and needs an action plan.

KPITargetActualVarianceStatus
Monthly Revenue$40,000$38,500-3.75%AMBER
Client Retention95%96%+1%GREEN
Quote Conversion40%28%-30%RED
Referrals Received4/month6+50%GREEN
  • 1

    Set your monthly review meeting now

    Block 90 minutes on the first Monday of every month. This is non-negotiable. Treat it like a client meeting.

  • 2

    Record actuals at end of each month

    Assign responsibility for collecting each metric. Many can be automated from your accounting software, CRM or Google Analytics.

  • 3

    Use red KPIs to drive action plans

    For every Red KPI: identify the cause, set an action, assign a deadline, review progress next month. A Red KPI without an action plan is just a problem you are aware of.

  • 4

    Share with your team

    Even if you have one staff member, sharing the scorecard builds accountability and helps them understand how their work connects to the business results.

Lesson 4 of 6

Setting Meaningful Targets

How to set KPI targets that stretch you without being fictional.

The Problem with Targets

Most business owners either set targets that are too easy (their comfort zone) or too ambitious (wishful thinking). Both are demotivating. The right target is 10–20% above your current performance — achievable with genuine effort and the right actions, but not guaranteed without them.

  • 1

    Start with your current baseline

    You cannot set a meaningful target without knowing your current actual. If you don't know your current conversion rate or retention rate, that is the first thing to measure.

  • 2

    Apply the 10–20% stretch rule

    Whatever your current number is, set this year's target at 10–20% better. Revenue: $320k actual → $370k target. Conversion: 28% actual → 33% target. Small consistent improvement compounds significantly over time.

  • 3

    Work backwards from your financial goal

    If you want $500k revenue and your average client pays $2,000/month, you need 21 clients (up from perhaps 14). To get 21 clients you need to acquire 10 new ones. To acquire 10 you need to convert how many proposals? To send that many proposals you need how many enquiries? Now you know your lead generation target.

  • 4

    Set quarterly milestones, not just annual targets

    Annual targets feel distant. Break them into quarterly milestones. By end of Q1: 16 clients. Q2: 18 clients. Q3: 20 clients. Q4: 21 clients. Now each quarter has a clear, near-term goal.

📍 The Revenue Bridge

Build your target from the bottom up

Revenue target: $480k
÷ Average client value: $2,000/month = 20 clients needed
Current clients: 14 → need to add 6 net new
Retention: 95% → lose ~1 per year → need 7 new clients
Conversion rate: 33% → need 21 proposals
Enquiry to proposal: 60% → need 35 enquiries
Monthly enquiries needed: 3
Now you know your lead generation target precisely.
