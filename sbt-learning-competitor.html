Lesson 1 of 6

Why Competitor Analysis Matters

Most businesses ignore their competitors until they start losing clients. Here's why that's a mistake.

The Business Intelligence Advantage

Understanding your competitors in detail gives you an edge in pricing, positioning, sales conversations and marketing. When a prospect says "your competitor does it cheaper," you need to know exactly why — and what that cheaper option does not include. Competitor analysis is not copying. It is intelligence.

What You Need to Know About Each Competitor

Five things matter most: What do they charge? What do they promise? What do their customers say (reviews)? Where are they weak? What are they not doing that you could?

💡 Research shows that 30% of new product ideas come from analysing competitors

Turn their weaknesses into your opportunities

Every competitor weakness is a potential market gap. Every negative review they receive is a brief you should be reading. Every service they don't offer is a potential product development opportunity. Systematic competitor analysis is one of the highest-return activities a small business can invest time in.

How Often Should You Review Competitors?

At minimum: quarterly. More often if you are in a fast-moving market. Set a Google Alert for each major competitor's name right now — you will be automatically notified when they appear in new content, reviews or news.

Lesson 2 of 6

How to Research Your Competitors

The specific methods and tools to build a detailed competitor profile — most of them free.

  • 1

    Start with their website

    Read it like a prospect. What do they lead with? What's their value proposition? What services do they list? What do they not mention? Note their pricing if visible and the language they use to describe their offer.

  • 2

    Read every Google review they have

    This is gold. Positive reviews tell you what their best clients value. Negative reviews tell you exactly where they are weak — and those weaknesses are your opportunities. Look for patterns across multiple reviews.

  • 3

    Check their social media

    How often do they post? What content performs well? Are they running ads? (Facebook Ad Library shows all active Facebook and Instagram ads for any business — free and publicly accessible.) What engagement do they get?

  • 4

    Get a quote from them

    This may feel uncomfortable but it is legitimate market research. Have someone (a friend, a family member) contact them as a prospect and get a full quote. You will learn their process, their pricing, their sales approach and their responsiveness.

  • 5

    Talk to their former clients

    If you win a client from a competitor, always ask: what made you switch? What were you unhappy with? What did they do well? This is your most valuable intelligence.

  • 6

    Check LinkedIn

    How large is their team? What skills do they hire for? Who are their key staff? Are they growing or have there been redundancies? This tells you a lot about their financial position and strategic direction.

🛠️ Free Tools to Use Right Now

No cost, immediate intelligence

Google: search "[competitor name] reviews" — check Google, Facebook, Trustpilot and industry sites.
Facebook Ad Library (facebook.com/ads/library): see all their active ads.
Google Alerts: set an alert for each competitor name — free ongoing monitoring.
SEMrush or Ubersuggest (free tiers): see what keywords they rank for and what ads they run.

Lesson 3 of 6

Positioning Against Competitors

How to use competitor intelligence to position your business so clients choose you.

The Positioning Map

A positioning map plots you and your competitors on two axes relevant to your market — typically price vs quality, or specialist vs generalist, or service level vs price. It makes your competitive position visual and helps you identify gaps where no competitor currently sits.

What to Do With Your Map

Once plotted, look for: (1) Where is there a gap — a position no competitor occupies? (2) Where are you clustered with competitors — meaning price and differentiation will determine who wins? (3) Is there a movement you could make to a less competitive position?

💡 The Differentiation Decision

Being in the middle of the map is the most dangerous position

Businesses clustered in the centre — medium price, medium specialisation — compete on price by default. The most successful positioning is either: premium specialist (highest margin, most loyal clients) or volume generalist (lowest price, high volume). The middle ground is where margins go to die.

Lesson 4 of 6

Using Competitor Intelligence in Sales

How to win deals when prospects compare you to competitors.

The Comparison Conversation

When a prospect says "Competitor X is cheaper," most business owners get defensive or discount their price. Neither works. The right approach is to welcome the comparison and use your competitor intelligence to show exactly what the difference in price represents. You cannot do this without having done the research.

  • 1

    Know their price before the meeting

    Your research should have given you competitor pricing. If you know Competitor A charges $1,500/month for SEO and you charge $2,200, you can prepare your response before the question is asked.

  • 2

    Know their weaknesses before the meeting

    If you know Competitor A gets negative reviews about reporting and communication, you can proactively address this: "One thing I hear from businesses who've tried other agencies is that they never know what's actually happening with their SEO. We solve that with a weekly report and a monthly strategy call."

  • 3

    Never badmouth — just contrast

    Don't say "Competitor X is terrible." Say "We do things differently from most agencies in three specific ways." This is professional, confident and more persuasive than criticism.

  • 4

    Price difference as a question, not a defence

    When they say "Competitor X is $700 cheaper," respond with "That's worth exploring. Can I ask — what does their reporting look like? How often do they meet with you?" These questions highlight your strengths without you having to boast.

  • 5

    Have client testimonials ready that address the comparison

    If you know prospects compare you to Competitor A, ask your best clients: "If someone were comparing us to another agency, what would you tell them?" Their words are more powerful than yours.

💡 Win Rate by Preparation

Businesses that research competitors before sales meetings convert at significantly higher rates

The reason is simple: they can answer comparison questions with specifics, not platitudes. "We're better" loses. "Our clients report X% more leads in the first 3 months, and here's why our approach is different" wins.
